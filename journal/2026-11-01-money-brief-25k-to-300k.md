# MONEY BRIEF — GENERAL ANALYSIS

**Question:** If you had $25,000, what is the best, fastest way to turn it
into $300,000?

Revision 1.0 · 2026-11-01 · Source prompt: `playbooks/2026-11-01-money-brief-prompt.md`.
This is general analysis, not personal financial, tax, or legal advice.

Evidence labels: **[D]** documented (founder's own book, filings, major
press) · **[R]** reported (secondary or self-reported) · **[I]** inference
from the evidence · **[?]** unverified.

## 1. The Answer in Three Sentences

1. None of the 10 founders turned a small stake into a large one by
   investing it. They used it to buy the one thing that let them make their
   first sale, and from then on **customers' money paid for the growth**.
2. 12x through investing takes about 26 years at 10% a year. Trying to do it
   fast through trading is a bet most individuals lose. The fast route is a
   high-priced service, paid for up front, sold in person by you.
3. Using the offer you already designed ($997 setup + $149 a month), $300,000
   in 12 months takes **about 14 new customers a month (168 total)**. Hold
   $10,000 of the $25,000 back, and spend nothing on growth until a
   20-door test gives you a real close rate.

**Popular myth these 10 cases do NOT support:** "Put your savings into
the market and let compounding make you rich fast." Compounding made Buffett
rich over 70 years. His fast early money came from operating a business
(the pinball machines, a paper route), not from investment returns.

## 4. The Capital Math

**Multiple:** $300,000 ÷ $25,000 = **12x**.

**Route A: invest it.** [I] At 10% a year (roughly the S&P 500's long-run
nominal average) 12x takes **26.1 years**. At 7% it takes **36.7 years**.
This route is not fast.

**Route B: trade or speculate.** [D] A study of Brazilian day traders who
traded for more than 300 days found **97% lost money**, and about 1% earned
more than the minimum wage (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for
a Living?", 2019). 12x in about a year needs repeated wins at high leverage.
That is a bet, not a plan.

**Route C: a business that customers fund.** This is what 8 of the 10
founders did (P1). Worked through the offer you have already designed
(prices locked in `handoffs/2026-08-07-guild-handoff.md`: $997 setup, $149 a
month, setup credited toward month one):

| New customers per month, for 12 months | Cash collected in 12 months | Total customers |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | $217,980 | 120 |
| **14** | **$305,172** | **168** |
| 16 (if 5% of customers cancel each month) | $300,000+ | 192 |
| 19 (if every sale is the $497 tier) | $300,000+ | 228 |
| 20 | $435,960 | 240 |

One customer signed in month 1 is worth **$2,636** in the first 12 months
($997 + 11 × $149).

**Conversations needed** [I]: at a 10% close rate, 14 customers a month
takes **140 conversations a month, about 7 every workday**. The close rate
is **unknown**. Nothing has been sold at these prices yet (read from papers:
Guild handoff dated 2026-08-07, section 1).

**What the $25,000 is for:** it does not get multiplied. It pays for time
and for finding customers, like Walton's $25,000 paid for the store.

| Part | Amount | Founder lesson behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Reserve, do not touch | $10,000 | Knight and John: growth uses up cash |
| Getting customers (door days, printed price cards, mail to consented or allowed lists) | $8,000, released only after Move 1 | Blakely: spend only on the bottleneck |
| Tools for 12 months (phone answering at $97/month = $1,164, AI usage, CRM) | $4,000 | Dell: keep fixed costs small |
| Contractor help with delivery once demand outruns you | $3,000 | Cuban: hire after the work exists, not before |
| **Total** | **$25,000** | |

**Revenue is not profit.** $300,000 collected is not $300,000 kept. The
revenue split across the seven Guild partners is still undecided (Guild
handoff, section 6), and that split decides your share.

## 6. Exactly Two Moves

**MOVE 1 — The 20-door price test (already designed in the Guild handoff,
section 5.5).**

- **Purpose:** turn C2 (close rate) from unknown into a number.
- **Founder lessons used:** Knight (sell in person first), Blakely (live
  demo at the moment of decision), Airbnb (do the unscalable thing).
- **Steps:** knock 20 doors. Quote $497 at 10 and $997 at 10. At each door,
  ask ChatGPT on your phone for "[their trade] near me" in front of the
  owner and show whether they come up.
- **Cost:** under $300 for printed price cards. About 5 working days.
- **Output:** closes at each price, plus every objection written down.
- **Reversible:** yes.
- **Stop condition:** 0 closes out of 20 at both prices means the offer or
  the pitch is wrong. Spend nothing more and rework the offer (Hormozi).

**MOVE 2 — Scale only at the price that closed. Depends on the Move 1
result.**

- **If 2 or more closes out of 10 at $997:** release the $8,000 for finding
  customers. Build up to 140 conversations a month. Collect payment before
  starting the work (Dell). Hire delivery help only once you have 10 or
  more paying customers (Cuban).
- **If only $497 closes:** the plan needs about 19 customers a month. Run 20
  more doors before spending the $8,000.
- **If fewer than 2 closes at either price:** do not release the money.
- **Stop condition:** any month where cash out is more than cash in, without
  a reason you can name.

## 7. What Not to Do Now

1. **Don't put any of the $25,000 into options, crypto, or day trading.**
   97% of persistent day traders lost money (section 4).
2. **Don't build new software before 10 paying customers.** Chestnut ran an
   agency for 6 years before going all-in on his product.
3. **Don't buy ads before you know your close rate.** Every founder above
   found their first customers in person (P2).
4. **Don't start work without payment up front.** Daymond John had $300,000
   in orders and almost lost his mother's house.
5. **Don't touch the $10,000 reserve to speed things up.** Knight's banks
   cut him off in the middle of growing.
6. **Don't build on a platform you don't control without a written
   agreement.** Walton lost his first store over a lease clause.

## 8. Risks and What Would Change This Answer

- **Survivorship bias:** all 10 founders are winners. Thousands of people
  did the same things and failed. This is why Move 1 costs under $300 and
  can be undone.
- **Strongest argument against Move 1:** 20 doors is a small sample. One
  lucky or unlucky day can skew it. If the result is borderline (1 close out
  of 10), run 20 more before deciding.
- **What would change the answer:**
  - A close rate under 5% moves the timeline past 12 months.
  - Your share of the seven-way split sets your real take.
  - If the $25,000 isn't real (C6), Move 1 still works, because it costs
    under $300.
